Manual

Everything underground is made of cells of matter, and every cell follows simple rules: it holds, piles, flows or drifts, it burns or it doesn't, and it changes when it touches certain other things. This manual lists every material and every interaction in the game as it stands, so you know what you are digging into.

How matter moves

Each material belongs to one of four families. The family decides how it moves; its density decides what it pushes aside.

The rules

Heavier sinks, lighter rises

Every moving material has a density. A heavier one swaps places with a lighter one below it: sand sinks through water, oil floats on it, quicksilver drops through almost everything. Gases lighter than air rise and gather under ceilings; heavier ones sink and pool in hollows.

Fire spreads by flammability

Fire, embers and lava try to light every neighbour each tick, with that neighbour's flammability as the chance. Methane catches at once; moss barely. What a thing turns into when it catches is listed on its card: fire, embers that smoulder for a long time, or an explosion.

Touching things react

Pairs of materials that touch can change, at a set chance per tick. Water dissolves salt, acid eats rock, lava meeting water sets into obsidian. Each pair is written once and works from either side. The full list is in the reaction index.

Explosions chain

Lit coal dust and hydrogen become a blast. A blast breaks weak rock into gravel, scatters powder, fills the air with fire and smoke, and sets off every other explosive within reach, so a pocket goes up all at once. You get thrown and hurt by how close you were.

Shale warns, then fails

Shale stays up while something holds it from below, or while it spans between solid rock on both sides like an arch. Cut it and the cells beside your cut lose their hold: they trickle grit for one to four seconds, then come down as gravel, which leaves the next cells unsupported. Collapses spread outward from where you dug.

Still places still change

Parts of the cave where nothing is moving go to sleep to save work, but every sixteen ticks they are swept for slow chemistry and growth. Salt keeps dissolving in a quiet pool, ice keeps creeping across still water, grey lace keeps spreading, and pockets of gas sit exactly where they are until something disturbs them.

Light comes only from glowing things

Nothing is lit unless something lights it: your lamp, a flare, fire, lava, embers, lantern moss, glow worms or crystal. Gas is invisible to the eye. Your suit sensor ticks when you breathe something bad, and that is the only warning you get.

Hands and picks

You can climb anything marked climbable, on stamina. Everything solid has a hardness that sets how many strikes it takes; bedrock and lava can't be dug at all. Striking hard rock and ore throws sparks, and sparks light gas and coal dust.

Chains to know

Most deaths underground will come from two or three rules lining up. These are the ones the cave is built to produce.

Strange matter

Some of what is down there shouldn't exist. Strange matter mostly obeys the same rules as everything else (cold freezes, heat boils, heavy sinks), but each kind has one thing it does that ordinary matter never would. The reasonable part tells you how to handle it; the surprise is what gets people killed.

Breathing

Your suit holds about two and a half minutes of air and only refills at the base. Whatever is at head height sets how fast it drains. Foul air and mercury vapour also damage your lungs, and steam scalds.
